The brief

Travel content often collapses into listicles and itineraries. Nomad starts from the journal: the small observations, wrong turns, and meals that make a trip memorable. The map is an index, not the main event.

The platform needed to support photo essays, short notes, and route overviews without forcing every entry into the same template. It also needed to load quickly on mobile connections.

A content model for memory

Each trip is a collection of entries. An entry can be a place, a day, or a standalone note. Places carry coordinates, a short description, and an optional photo set. Routes connect places in order, but the reading view does not depend on the map.

This model lets an author publish a single entry before a trip is complete, then gradually build the route. It also makes the archive useful months later, when the map matters less than the writing.

Editorial interface

The reading layout uses a generous measure, large captions, and a quiet palette that lets photography lead. A sticky route rail on wide screens shows where the reader is without interrupting the text. On small screens, the rail becomes a collapsible summary.

Images are lazy-loaded with explicit dimensions. The map is rendered only when a reader asks for it, which keeps the initial page light and avoids loading a heavy mapping library for every visit.

Build notes

The front end is a static build with small progressive enhancements: reading progress, route filtering, and a lightbox for photo sets. Everything essential works without JavaScript.

A production version would need an editorial CMS, image processing, and a moderation workflow. The concept focuses on the reading experience and the content model that would support it.
