The brief

Health tracking can quickly become another job. Pulse asks what happens if the product only asks for the smallest useful piece of information each day, then shows patterns without implying blame or diagnosis.

The concept focuses on energy, mood, sleep, and movement. It is not a medical product and does not offer treatment advice. The interface is explicit about that boundary.

Research synthesis

I mapped common friction points from public product reviews and wellbeing research: too many inputs, unclear privacy, and dashboards that prioritise trends over meaning. Those themes became three design rules: ask less, explain more, and make the data portable.

The information architecture is intentionally shallow. Today, Patterns, and Settings are the only top-level destinations. Each check-in can be completed with four taps and an optional note.

Designing for trust

The visual language is warm and low-pressure. Data is shown as a short narrative first, with charts available underneath. Copy avoids clinical language and never labels a day as good or bad.

Privacy controls are written in plain language, with a single switch for pattern insights and a clear explanation of what is stored on the device. Export and delete actions are available from the same screen.
